Course Description and Objectives:

Course Description:

This graduate-level course provides a comprehensive introduction to healthcare business analytics, progressing from basic statistical concepts to practical applications of data analytics in healthcare management decisions. As healthcare organizations increasingly rely on data-driven insights, this course prepares students to navigate and analyze complex healthcare data systems, including electronic health records, claims databases, clinical registries, and population health surveys. Students will gain hands-on experience with modern analytical tools while learning to address real-world healthcare management challenges through evidence-based decision-making.

Course Objectives:

  • Understand the role of analytics in modern healthcare management
  • Analyze different types of healthcare data (claims, electronic health records, claims data, surveys, etc.)
  • Apply appropriate statistical methods to healthcare management problems to extract meaningful insights
  • Interpret and communicate analytical findings to stakeholders
  • Develop data-driven solutions for healthcare management challenges

R and R-Studio:

This course utilizes R and R-Studio as the primary analytical tools. R is a powerful, open-source programming language specifically designed for statistical data analysis, widely used in both academia and industry for analytics. R-Studio is an integrated development environment (IDE) that makes R programming more accessible and efficient through its user-friendly interface, offering features such as data visualization, code editing, debugging, and project management capabilities. Students will learn to use these tools to analyze healthcare data, create visualizations, develop statistical models, and generate reports. You can download R and R-Studio from the following links:

  • R: https://www.r-project.org
  • R-Studio: https://posit.co/downloads/

Assignments: 50% of final grade (12 assignments)

There will be 12 assignments. Each of the 12 assignments requires students to apply the analytical methods learned in class to new healthcare data analytics scenarios. Assignments are due weekly by Sunday at 11:59 PM.

Class Participation: 10% of final grade

Class participation will be evaluated through attendance tracking and participation in class activities. After each class, students will be asked to submit their work on Canvas, and the submissions will be used for class participation and attendance grading. The deadline for class activity submissions is within 30 minutes after class ends (e.g., for a class ending at 12:00 PM, submissions are due by 12:30 PM).

Midterm Exam: 20% of final grade

There will be a midterm exam in Week 9 of the semester. The exam will cover topics covered from Week 1 through Week 8. The exam will be online, and students need to complete within 24 hours.

Final Exam: 20% of final grade

There will be a final exam in the last week of the semester. The exam will be cumulative and cover what we cover in class. The exam will be online, and students need to complete within 24 hours.

Class Policies

Attendance Policy:

This course is delivered online and attendance is required. An attendance sheet will be circulated each class session for students to sign, documenting their presence.

Classroom Etiquette:

  • TURN OFF or MUTE cell phones during class.
  • Treat class time as a meeting in current/future professional life.
  • Show respect for yourself, your peers, and our school; behave in a civil and appropriate manner for a school of business.
  • FOOD is not permitted in the classroom. Coffee or beverages are allowed only in sealed containers. Students consuming food will be asked to step outside until finished.
  • Professional language is required in all class discussions and written assignments.

Technological Problems:

  • Students are responsible for ensuring reliable internet connectivity when submitting assignments.
  • All students must maintain an active UD email account and Canvas access.
  • Technical issues with personal computers or software (including R and R-Studio) are the student’s responsibility.
  • For hardware issues, contact your device manufacturer. For software issues, contact UD IT services. I will provide guidance on course-specific technical matters when possible.

Late Submissions:

A late submission is subject to the following policy. - In-class activity must be submitted on time; late submissions are not accepted. If you cannot submit work on time, you have to inform me immediately after class. Email notifications or after-deadline communications about late work are not accepted. - For other assignments, a 20% grade deduction applies for each 24-hour period past the deadline. Assignments submitted more than 48 hours late will receive zero points.

Retaining Assignments and Research Exercises:

  • Students must keep a copy of each Assignment or Research Exercise, and any related documents. In the unlikely event that an Assignment or Research Exercise is misplaced or cannot be found, the onus is on you to provide a backup copy of the assignment.
  • In addition, you must keep your graded assignments. Any queries concerning the awarded grade (e.g., a discrepancy between what is posted on Canvas and what is on your paper) can only be resolved with the original assignment document.

Check Grades:

Students are responsible for regularly monitoring their grades on Canvas. If you think an assignment that you handed in was not graded, or that the grade that was posted is wrong, feel free to reach out to me for review and verification.

Academic Honesty

You are expected to do your own written work on all assignments and projects. For specific UD policies concerning academic honesty, see the University’s Academic Honor Code in the Academic Catalog.

Contract Cheating

Contract cheating occurs when a student pays someone (such as a person or company found online) to write essays or complete assignments for a class. This defeats the purpose of the course, and I encourage you to follow the academic honor code and seek to develop a personal sense of academic integrity which lays the foundation for an ethical life. Contract cheating is especially dangerous because of the risk of bribery and extortion (at any point after after college.) Students are often lured into contract cheating by signing up for online tutoring services. Remember that UD provides many learning support resources through the Office of Learning Resources.

Academic Integrity and ChatGPT/AI Generated Content

As a student at UD, you are expected to adhere to the Standards of Conduct outlined in the Academic Honor Code. One of the goals for your educational development is to develop your critical thinking and writing skills, which can be weakened by regularly utilizing AI for writing. Thus, for this course, all submitted writing should be your own, original work. If you quote or copy content from ChatGPT or other AI sources, it must be appropriately cited in your submissions. As your instructor, I am required to investigate all suspected violations of academic dishonesty and follow the honor code violation procedures, which can result in an NC for the course.
