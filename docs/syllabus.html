Course Description and Objectives:

Course Description:

This graduate-level course provides a comprehensive introduction to business analytics in healthcare management, progressing from basic statistical concepts and data visualization to practical applications of data analytics in healthcare management contexts. As healthcare organizations increasingly rely on data-driven insights, this course prepares students to navigate and analyze complex healthcare datasets. Students will gain hands-on experience with classic and modern analytical tools while learning to address real-world healthcare management challenges through evidence-based decision-making.

Prerequisites:

There are no prerequisites for this course. This course is designed to accommodate students with varying levels of statistical and programming backgrounds. All necessary concepts will be introduced from the ground up.

Course Objectives:

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

  • Evaluate the role and application of analytics in healthcare management decision-making
  • Conduct exploratory data analysis on different types of healthcare data (patient surveys, health outcomes, etc.)
  • Select and apply appropriate statistical methods to healthcare management problems to extract meaningful insights
  • Interpret analytical results and communicate findings effectively to diverse stakeholders
  • Design and implement data-driven solutions for real-world healthcare management challenges

R and R-Studio:

This course utilizes R and R-Studio as the primary analytical tools. R is a powerful, open-source programming language specifically designed for statistical data analysis, widely used in both academia and industry for analytics. R-Studio is an integrated development environment (IDE) that makes R programming more accessible and efficient through its user-friendly interface, offering features such as data visualization, code editing, debugging, and project management capabilities. Students will learn to use these tools to analyze healthcare data, create visualizations, develop statistical models, and generate reports. You can download R and R-Studio from the following links:

  • R: https://www.r-project.org
  • R-Studio: https://posit.co/downloads/

You should install R first since R-Studio runs on R.

Assignments: 90 points (9 assignments, each with 10 points)

There will be 9 assignments throughout the semester. Each assignment requires students to apply the analytical methods learned in the class session to new healthcare data analytics scenarios. For example, in Week 4, after we cover data visualization, you will be asked to visualize results with a new dataset. Assignments will focus on different healthcare management contexts and typically take 30-60 minutes to complete. Assignments are due weekly by Sunday at 11:59 PM.

Class Participation and Reflection: 78 points

This course includes 14 class sessions. After we finish each session (starting from the second class, meaning you will submit 13 reflections total), I would like to hear about what you learned and what questions remain unclear. To encourage engagement with the course contents, you will need to write a short list of responses to the following questions:

  1. What were the three most interesting or exciting things you learned from the session? and why?
  2. What were the three most unclear or confusing things from the session? and why?

You can include more than three for each, but you must include at least three per question. So, there are six interesting or unclear things you will have in each reflection, and each one will be worth 1 point (total 6 points per class session; max is 6 points). There will be 13 reflection assignments throughout the semester (6 points × 13 reflections = 78 points). Reflection submissions are due weekly by Sunday at 11:59 PM.

You don’t need to get stressed about this. Your reflections do not need to be fancy or very long (just one short paragraph for each point). I simply want to understand what genuinely resonated with you or what felt challenging. For example, you might write something like: “Learning how to draw a bar graph using ggplot was interesting. I didn’t know how to draw graphs in R before, so discovering this was exciting.” Or you could say: “Understanding the interaction effect was confusing. It’s unclear to me whether it means the effect of A is different from the effect of B, or whether the effect of A changes depending on B.” These kinds of honest, straightforward reflections are exactly what I’m looking for. The goal is to help me understand your learning experience so I can better support you throughout the course.

Midterm Exam: 50 points

There will be a midterm exam in Week 9 of the semester. The exam will cover topics from Week 1 through Week 8. The exam will be online and available for the full day on the scheduled class day (from 12:00 AM to 11:59 PM).

  • Hold onto your graded work as well. If you ever have questions about a grade (e.g., a mismatch between Canvas and your feedback), having the original document will help us resolve it quickly.

Stay on Top of Your Grades:

Academic Honesty

You are expected to do your own written work on all assignments and projects. For specific UD policies concerning academic honesty, see the University’s Academic Honor Code in the Academic Catalog.

Contract Cheating

Contract cheating occurs when a student pays someone (such as a person or company found online) to write essays or complete assignments for a class. This defeats the purpose of the course, and I encourage you to follow the academic honor code and seek to develop a personal sense of academic integrity which lays the foundation for an ethical life. Contract cheating is especially dangerous because of the risk of bribery and extortion (at any point after after college.) Students are often lured into contract cheating by signing up for online tutoring services. Remember that UD provides many learning support resources through the Office of Learning Resources.

Academic Integrity and ChatGPT/AI Generated Content

As a student at UD, you are expected to adhere to the Standards of Conduct outlined in the Academic Honor Code. One of the goals for your educational development is to develop your critical thinking and writing skills, which can be weakened by regularly utilizing AI for writing. Thus, for this course, all submitted writing should be your own, original work. If you quote or copy content from ChatGPT or other AI sources, it must be appropriately cited in your submissions. As your instructor, I am required to investigate all suspected violations of academic dishonesty and follow the honor code violation procedures, which can result in an NC for the course.
